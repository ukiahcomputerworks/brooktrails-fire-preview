Website redesign proposal · Prepared for Town Manager Tamara Alaniz

From a maze of pages to a place worth exploring.

Brooktrails already had the information. The redesign gives it shape, atmosphere, and momentum—so a resident can complete a task, a future neighbor can understand the community, and neither has to learn the organization chart first.

The transformation

The old site made useful information feel harder than it was.

Thirty legacy routes, long menus, repeated destinations, generic document lists, and desktop-oriented layouts made a modest body of civic information feel enormous. Specific-looking links often landed on generic pages, while residents had to decide whether a need belonged under services, planning, resources, government, or contact.

Before: information exposure

  • Overlapping navigation and repeated classification choices
  • Important forms mixed into long, cryptically named document lists
  • History, operations, and visitor tasks competing at the same level
  • Long pages and oversized elements that did not fit smaller screens
  • A visitor could reach information without feeling oriented

After: guided discovery

  • Four bounded hubs with no duplicate purpose
  • Searchable records and District Desk treated as utilities
  • Exact links open the promised panel, form, map, or source
  • Layouts tested across compact laptops, desktop, and phones
  • Every choice produces a visible, useful next layer

The design flow

Limited hangouts keep attention focused.

A limited hangout is a deliberately bounded place to pause, understand one subject, and choose the next useful depth. Instead of unloading the whole sitemap, the site reveals information in satisfying stages.

01 · Arrive

A cinematic redwood-and-water identity immediately says where you are and what the site is for.

02 · Recognize

Four distinct visual cues let the visitor choose by intent, not by district structure.

03 · Engage

A clear clickable question, trail, docket, current, or compass invites action.

04 · Reveal

The chosen panel enumerates the useful facts, steps, maps, records, or story.

05 · Continue

An exact next step leads to the right form, official agency, record, or person.

The engagement grammar

Every important element earns attention.

The site uses small, ethical interaction rewards—not dark patterns—to make civic information feel inviting. Motion is restrained, keyboard access is preserved, and reduced-motion users receive the information without animation.

Draw

The visual signal: moving water lines, a trail sign, layered public docket, turning compass, illuminated field card, or a golfer preparing to swing.

Hook

The meaningful action: a question, tab, search suggestion, springboard, map, or clearly labeled button that promises a specific result.

Reward

The useful reveal: numbered steps, exact records, a story sequence, phone number, form, map, or the next responsible action. Curiosity pays off immediately.

Four hubs · four experiences

The destinations feel different because the visits are different.

The primary navigation and homepage now agree. Each hub owns one kind of visit and uses its own interaction metaphor.

Two people · two clear paths

Useful to the citizen. Inviting to the visitor.

The same architecture supports a five-minute civic task and an open-ended exploration without making either person pass through the other’s information.

The citizen looking for a form

Arrive: search in ordinary language—“sewer lateral,” “park rental,” “building,” or “water report”—or enter through Services.

Follow: autocomplete suggests the exact task; contextual links open the correct document shelf, panel, or guided process instead of a generic resources page.

Finish: the record has a readable title, useful context, a direct download, and a currentness warning. If the answer is property-specific, changing, or sensitive, District Desk is the immediate fallback.

Deeper example: “Build or improve property” becomes a seven-step journey separating district design review from County permits, access, septic review, utilities, and final inspection.

The visitor discovering the area

Arrive: the water-and-redwoods hero establishes a sense of place before asking for a choice.

Follow: the compass and forest trailhead lead into history, maps, lakes, trails, conservation, gathering places, and golf—not administrative menus.

Explore: each click reveals a short story, numbered facts, photographs, a map, a trail resource, or a playful interaction such as the tee-shot phone reveal.

Go further: exact links turn interest into a real outing or deeper understanding while safety, services, and jurisdiction remain one search away. A future resident can also open the focused “Considering Brooktrails?” guide before making a property decision.

Less friction · more trust

Utilities stay available without becoming more hubs.

Upper-right search suggests answers while a visitor types—including everyday terms such as permit, moving, sewer availability, roads, insurance, and evacuation. The document library uses human-readable titles and preselected shelves. The District Desk consolidates phone, email, fax, and addresses when self-service is not appropriate.

One form was intentionally withheld.

The captured construction-meter application requested Social Security and driver’s-license information. Republishing it—and encouraging ordinary email submission—would create avoidable identity, retention, and records-handling risk. The preview instead directs applicants to request the current form and approved secure delivery method. The source remains recoverable for authorized review.

Recommended decision

Approve the direction, then prepare a controlled production migration.

The preview demonstrates the information architecture, mobile experience, visual identity, search, and engagement model. It does not claim district acceptance or operate permits, payments, emergency systems, or sensitive-data intake.

  • Approve the four-hub architecture and visual direction.
  • Name a district content owner to confirm current contacts, fees, forms, dates, and policies.
  • Decide the secure intake method and minimize sensitive information collected.
  • Scope production hosting, accessibility acceptance, analytics, maintenance, support, rollback, and launch separately.
