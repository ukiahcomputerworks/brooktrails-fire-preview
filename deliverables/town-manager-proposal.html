Proposal review brief · Prepared for Town Manager Tamara Alaniz

A Brooktrails website built around decisions.

The redesign keeps the redwood character that makes Brooktrails memorable while giving residents, prospective residents, and reviewers short, trustworthy paths to the next useful action.

Proposal outcome

Replace a document-first experience with four bounded civic hubs, two high-intent guided journeys, a searchable public library, and a concise District Desk—without implying that the preview is an operating permit, payment, emergency, or records-delivery system.

What changed—and why

Four non-overlapping hubs

Services, Parks & Places, District & Board, and Discover Brooktrails each own a distinct kind of visit.

Why: Visitors stop encountering the same destinations under several labels, reducing the feeling that the district has more departments and pages than it actually does.

Build on My Property

A seven-step sequence distinguishes district design review from County permitting and later utility work.

Why: A resident should know which form starts the process, which agency decides each step, and what approval comes next.

Considering Brooktrails?

A practical orientation brings services, costs, maps, safety, recreation, and jurisdiction into one short decision guide.

Why: The previous journey sold the landscape but made a prospective resident assemble essential property questions from several unrelated pages.

Intent-aware search

Search recognizes terms such as permit, moving, sewer availability, roads, insurance, and evacuation, and offers recovery when no record matches.

Why: People search in their own language. A silent blank result can incorrectly suggest that the district has no information.

Human-readable records

Cryptic captured filenames are presented with descriptive titles, categories, dates where known, and a currentness warning.

Why: Clear labels improve confidence and reduce accidental downloads without rewriting the underlying public record.

Precise destinations

Map, planning, archive, and contact links now retain the visitor’s context rather than falling back to a generic page.

Why: A link should complete the promise made by its label; generic landings force visitors to restart their search.

The guided building journey

01Confirm parcel and district services
02Prepare plans and site information
03Submit district design review
04Receive written district approval
05Apply for County permits
06Complete access or septic review
07Coordinate utilities and final inspection

Why this order: It prevents a later-stage water-meter application or a County permit page from appearing to be the first step, while making the agency handoff explicit.

Why the legacy identity-data form was removed

The captured construction-meter application requested Social Security and driver’s-license information. Republishing that form in a public preview—and inviting ordinary email submission—would create unnecessary privacy, identity-theft, retention, and records-handling risk. The preview now tells applicants to request the current form and approved delivery method directly from district staff. The source remains recoverable in repository history for authorized review, but it is no longer published or searchable.

Recommended production decision: determine whether those fields are still necessary, apply data minimization, and provide a documented secure or in-person intake channel before republishing any equivalent form.

How the “limited hangout” architecture works

Arrival

The homepage offers two urgent journeys and four visually distinct hubs instead of a long sitemap.

Focused choice

Each hub reveals only related questions, keeping cognitive load low and curiosity high.

Useful depth

Exact forms, records, maps, and official outside agencies appear only when they help complete the selected task.

Human fallback

The District Desk remains one step away when the answer is current, property-specific, or sensitive.

What this proposal does not claim

  • The preview is not district acceptance or a production authorization.
  • Captured documents do not prove that fees, contacts, policies, or dates remain current.
  • The site does not issue permits, receive sensitive applications, process payments, or operate emergency services.
  • Production hosting, accessibility acceptance, records ownership, secure forms, analytics, maintenance, and support require a separately approved implementation plan.
Suggested review decision

Approve the information architecture and visual direction; identify a district content owner; confirm current forms and fees; decide secure intake requirements; and scope a controlled production migration.
