# Design and content decisions

The redesign treats Brooktrails as one public-service district organized around resident tasks rather than organization structure. On September 29, owner direction separated the Fire Department into its own forthcoming website. The district preview therefore retains only the universal 911 and county-alert utility strip; Fire navigation, cards, contacts, and destination pages are absent from the primary experience. Captured Fire source material remains available in the retained archive for migration continuity.

The September 29 revision makes an experiential leap under the name **Redwood Afterglow**. It uses the emotional logic of a rewarding, destination-quality experience without copying the Alpha After Dark look. The visual language belongs to Brooktrails: nocturnal redwood greens, creek-light cyan, lantern gold, warm mist text, and topographic linework. Architectural sans serif headlines create a distinctive trail-marker voice, with selective serif phrasework adding a softer sense of place; plain system sans serif text keeps operational information easy to scan.

The home page is now a field guide rather than an agency landing page. It has four distinct, non-card gateways: Services is a flowing water current, Parks & Places is a trailhead signpost, District & Board is an opening public docket, and Discover Brooktrails is a compass. Primary navigation mirrors those four hubs exactly, with Home as the return point. Search records and forms plus the District Desk are utilities, not competing hubs.

The site-wide search occupies the upper-right masthead on desktop and remains available beside the mobile menu. Suggestions populate as the visitor types and combine hubs, direct answers, utilities, guided journeys, and 47 district-facing records. Search vocabulary includes visitor language such as permit, moving, sewer availability, insurance, and evacuation. Keyboard arrows move through results, Enter follows the active suggestion, Escape closes the list, and a visible zero-result state sends free-form submissions to the document library.

Parks & Places, District & Board, and Discover use a shared field-card dashboard. A numbered choice rail opens one adjacent information card with concise steps and relevant actions. Employment is now an explicit seventh civic question. The structure is informed by the successful Alpha regulations interaction, while color, language, motion, and materials remain distinctly Brooktrails. The document library uses seven plain-language shelves and search instead of displaying 51 filenames at once. Fire-only files do not appear in district-facing search.

Specific promises now open specific destinations: meeting links can open the meeting panel, trail concerns reach the District Desk, Ordinance 63 and the origin story open the matching retained-source section, and the Water page has a four-part jump rail. The District Desk ends after the consolidated contact channels instead of sending visitors back through a redundant hub grid.

Migration accounting is no longer resident content. The legacy 30-route table and its review caveats are generated as `deliverables/content-migration-review.html`; the full source archive remains available for evidence and continuity.

Two intent-based journeys cross the four hubs without becoming competing navigation sections. **Build on My Property** presents the district-to-county handoff as seven ordered steps and states that district design review is not a County building permit. **Considering Brooktrails?** answers the practical pre-purchase questions the narrative experience did not: service availability, costs, buildability, safety, recreation, jurisdiction, and the value of bringing an address or assessor’s parcel number.

The captured construction-meter form requested Social Security and driver’s-license information. It is no longer linked, indexed, or present in the publishable preview repository. The site instead tells applicants to request the current form and approved submission method from the District Desk and warns against emailing identity documents. This is a privacy and records-handling correction, not an assertion that the district’s current operational form has been reviewed.

The formal town-manager proposal is generated as `deliverables/town-manager-proposal.html`. It pairs each design change with its resident, trust, or risk rationale and includes the bounded-hub flow and guided building journey for review by Tamara Alaniz.

Motion is progressive enhancement. Elements reveal only when JavaScript, viewport observation, and the visitor's motion preference allow it. Pointer lighting is decorative and does not carry meaning. Keyboard focus, touch targets, reading order, and print output remain explicit parts of the system.

Changing facts are framed carefully. Meeting materials, connection availability, employment openings, and office-holder information direct readers to confirm with the district or responsible public agency. The universal safety strip calls 911 directly and links to county alerts. The preview does not simulate live alerts, payment processing, or official forms.

All locally retained source images and documents are used only for this `noindex` redesign preview. Fire assets remain preserved locally but are not used by the district-facing experience. Third-party historical reproductions should receive a rights review before any production launch. Production `btcsd.org`, forms, payments, emergency systems, and DNS remain outside this preview's authority.
