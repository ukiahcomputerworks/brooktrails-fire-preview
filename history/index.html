Story 01 · Origins

From working ranch to forest community.

Brooktrails grew from the former Diamond D Ranch northwest of Willits. In the late 1960s, plans paired homes and infrastructure with a large conservation dedication.

  1. Ranch landscape. The Diamond D Ranch anchors the early story.
  2. Planned community. Development brought roads, homes, and utility questions.
  3. Local district. Public services evolved through improvement districts into a Community Services District.

Story 02 · The promise

Development made room for a living forest.

Approximately 2,500 acres were dedicated for conservation and recreation, becoming the foundation of Brooktrails Redwood Park.

  1. Dedication. Conservation land was woven into the original community plan.
  2. Establishment. Ordinance 63 formally created the park in 1988.
  3. Stewardship. Later plans shaped long-term greenbelt care.

Story 03 · Water

Two lakes, one connected system.

The district describes Lake Emily feeding Lake Ada Rose before water moves through treatment, storage, pumps, and roughly 60 miles of mains.

  1. Collect. Surface water is held in two local reservoirs.
  2. Treat and store. The system prepares water and maintains tank storage.
  3. Move uphill. Electric pump stations make conservation especially important during outages.

Story 04 · Then and now

See the community through its surviving images.

Historical photographs, newspaper coverage, and a 1939 railroad brochure preserve different views of the landscape before and during Brooktrails' development.

  1. Look closely. Historic images preserve buildings, terrain, and promotion.
  2. Read in context. Period documents reflect the language and priorities of their time.
  3. Compare with today. The lakes and forest remain central to community identity.
